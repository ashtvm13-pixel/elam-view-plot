What the sheet can and cannot fix

The sheet does not determine the shape on its own. Six side lengths plus one area is seven numbers; a hexagon has eight shape degrees of freedom. One degree of freedom is left over, so infinitely many different-looking hexagons carry these exact six lengths and this exact area. The sheet records no bearings and no corner coordinates, so the missing piece has to come from somewhere else.

What pins it here. Three of the six lines are road frontage, and the road is visible on the imagery. Seating F–G–H–C on the road — with H on the inside corner of the bend — fixes that spare degree of freedom from the ground rather than from a guess. The result holds all six lengths to the centimetre and returns 2 442.36 m² exactly.

Still approximate. Absolute position rests on the Elam view point pin and a screenshot scale of 0.136 m/px, so the whole figure could sit some metres off true. Ground height is SRTM 30 m — right for the hillside trend, not a levelled survey. For anything legal or for cut-and-fill, get the surveyor's bearings or corner coordinates; they would replace the one assumption this model still makes.
